Before You Go

Documents, phones, insurance + money

Do I need a passport or visa?

Yes, you need a valid passport. U.S. citizens traveling to the Dominican Republic for a short tourist visit generally do not need to obtain a tourist visa in advance.

You will also need to complete the Dominican Republic's free electronic E-Ticket for entry. We recommend completing it online about 48–72 hours before departure so it isn't something you're scrambling to do at the airport.

Don't worry about remembering — we'll send reminders and additional instructions closer to departure.

What's an eSIM, and do I actually need one?

An eSIM gives your phone mobile data in the Dominican Republic without needing to swap your physical SIM card. It isn't mandatory, but we strongly recommend having reliable international data for WhatsApp, maps, Uber, group messages, and contacting everyone away from the villa.

Before buying one, check your current mobile plan. Your carrier may already include international roaming or offer a temporary international upgrade. Just make sure you also check how well your provider covers the Sosúa / Cabarete area before relying on it instead of an eSIM.

If your normal plan isn't a good option, an eSIM is usually an easy and relatively inexpensive alternative.

Do I need travel insurance?

It isn't required, but we recommend considering it. Travel insurance can help with things like trip cancellation or interruption, significant delays, lost baggage, or other unexpected travel problems.

We've included a conservative planning estimate in Costs, but you're completely free to shop around and choose whatever coverage makes sense for you.

What about international health insurance?

Your normal U.S. health insurance may provide limited — or no — coverage internationally, so it's worth checking your policy before departure.

Short-term travel medical coverage is usually relatively inexpensive for a trip this short and can provide additional protection if you unexpectedly need medical care while abroad.

Should I bring cash?

Yes, some! Dominican pesos are especially useful for smaller shops, beach vendors, tips, family-owned businesses, and other purchases where cards may not be the easiest option.

You don't need to carry your entire vacation budget around in cash. We've put more information about pesos, ATMs, exchange rates, and getting local currency under Packing.

What should I pack?

Passport. Phone. Medications. Clothes. House keys so you can still get into your home after the trip.

Beyond the essentials, there are activity-specific things you'll want — including water shoes for the waterfalls and clothes you don't mind getting dirty for the ATVs.

And gentlemen: do not forget your thrifted shirt for the arrival exchange.

Flights + RSVP

Getting there, paying + confirming your spot

Why are we using group travel?

The biggest reason: everyone gets there together.

Coordinating the flights means we can arrive in the DR as one group instead of making multiple airport transfers, tracking several arrival times, or leaving people waiting while another group makes its connection.

Group travel also gives everyone a guaranteed seat at the locked-in group rate, provides months to save toward the ticket without watching airfare fluctuate, and lets us coordinate travelers from Austin, Phoenix, and San Francisco onto the same flight into Santiago.

And you don't have to hunt for flights yourself. Win-win.

How does flight payment work?

A $100 deposit is due September 9, 2026, and your remaining flight balance is due March 8, 2027.

Payments are made to us through Venmo or Zelle. The payment-plan options under Flights aren't financing agreements — they're simply optional reminder schedules if you'd rather mentally divide the balance into smaller payments.

You can otherwise pay the remaining balance whenever you're ready.

Can I book my own return flight?

Your group airfare already includes your round-trip flights, so you do not need to purchase a separate return ticket.

If you're interested in extending your stay in the Dominican Republic, talk to us! We may be able to coordinate something with you. However, changing your return itinerary may mean you can no longer book as part of the group reservation, which can affect both pricing and scheduling.

Please talk to us before booking anything separately so we can figure out the best option together.

When do I need to RSVP by?

Please RSVP by September 9, 2026.

Our flight arrangements, villa rooms, activities, transportation, and other reservations will all be based on the confirmed group, so the earlier we know, the easier it is for us to plan.

Can I join after the RSVP deadline?

Maybe! Contact us as soon as possible and we'll see what can still be accommodated.

Rooms, activities, and transportation will also depend on reservations we've already made, so we can't guarantee late additions — but we'll do our best.

The RSVP will include a place to tell us if there's someone you'd like to bring who isn't currently on the guest list. If we have space available closer to the payment deadline, we'll reach out.

Can I extend my trip?

Potentially! We'd love for you to enjoy more of the DR if you have the time.

Just talk to us before changing anything involving your group flight, since altering the itinerary can affect the group reservation, pricing, schedule, and airport transportation.

Sosúa + Cabarete

Weather, transportation + exploring

What's Sosúa / Cabarete like?

We're staying between Sosúa and Cabarete on the Dominican Republic's north coast. The area is known for beautiful beaches, surfing and kiteboarding, restaurants, nightlife, and a large international tourism community.

Like anywhere you travel, use good judgment when arranging your own transportation and avoid unnecessarily flashing expensive valuables. Think normal big-city awareness, not “we need a buddy system to cross the street.”

What's the weather going to be like?

Warm, tropical, and raining men. Hallelujah.

Actual forecast: expect warm daytime temperatures, warm evenings, plenty of humidity and tropical sun, and the possibility of brief rain showers.

We've included a more useful historical weather breakdown — and what that means for packing — under Packing.

How do I get around during free time?

Uber operates in the area, although availability can be less consistent around Sosúa and Cabarete than in major city centers.

We're working on identifying additional trusted private drivers and transportation services before the trip. For anything we're doing as an organized group, we'll arrange transportation whenever it's needed.

What happens if I need medical care?

We've already identified several medical options in the Sosúa / Cabarete area, including 24/7 emergency facilities, daytime clinics, and larger hospitals in Puerto Plata.

The Villa map includes the locations and general level of care so nobody has to start researching from scratch if something happens.
